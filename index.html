ABOUT ME

About me

I am a PhD candidate in Materials Science and Engineering at Clemson University, working with Dr. Olga Kuksenok. My research focuses on computational materials design and polymer physics, including phase separation in block copolymer blends incorporating polymer rings and dynamic heterogeneity during hydrogel degradation.

I use Dissipative Particle Dynamics (DPD) with modified Segmental Repulsive Potential (mSRP), alongside unsupervised learning, to study polymer systems. My earlier research spans deep learning for ceramic image analysis and radiative cooling optimization.

I am interested in research scientist and postdoctoral opportunities in computational materials science, polymer physics, and machine learning for materials.

01

MAY 2025 – PRESENT · CLEMSON

Phase separation in block copolymer blends

I investigate phase separation dynamics in block copolymer blends incorporating polymer rings, using DPD with mSRP and UMAP to characterize phase behavior and local and global data structures.

  • DPD / mSRP
  • UMAP
  • Unsupervised learning

Advisor: Dr. Olga Kuksenok

02

AUG 2023 – APR 2025 · CLEMSON

Dynamics of degrading hydrogels

I characterized dynamic heterogeneities during controlled hydrogel degradation using DPD with mSRP and quantified spatial fluctuations in local dynamic behavior.

  • Polymer dynamics
  • DPD / mSRP
  • Hydrogels
Read the Soft Matter paper

Advisor: Dr. Olga Kuksenok

03

AUG 2021 – JUL 2023 · CLEMSON

Deep learning for micro-structured images of sintered materials

I studied deep learning methods for analyzing pore and grain size distributions in sintered ceramic images and predicting processing parameters for additively manufactured materials using generative adversarial and convolutional neural networks.

  • Deep learning
  • Image analysis
  • Additive manufacturing

Advisor: Dr. Ulf D. Schiller

EARLIER RESEARCH · GIST · SEP 2019 – JUL 2021

Radiative cooling and photonic materials

Research in radiative cooling, thin films, photonics, coatings, and solar cells at the Flexible OptoElectronics Laboratory. My master’s thesis explored multilayer selective passive daytime radiative cooler optimization using a memetic algorithm, supervised by Dr. Young Min Song.
